ABOUT ME

Hi! I’m Lucy —
the one making
all of this ↙

I’m a senior at Saint James School in Montgomery, Alabama. I work across cut paper, collage, acrylic, watercolor, pen, and graphite — usually starting from something ordinary, like a cupcake, a lantern, or a pile of shapes, and pushing it until it says something about how we see.

Growing up between cultures, I’ve noticed how often people see only parts of who we are. A lot of my work comes back to that: fragments, contrast, and the details you miss at first glance.

Lesson & materials
Lesson
Paper-Cut Value Study
Size
12 × 18 in
Materials
Fadeless colored paper (12 × 18 in, 3–4 values), rubber cement, art knife, cutting board, pencil, #2 ebony pencil, newspaper, tape, photo reference
Process
  1. Found a photo reference in a newspaper or magazine.
  2. Chose a horizontal or vertical format.
  3. Made a format window in proportion to the Fadeless paper.
  4. Found and drew the light, medium, and dark value shapes within the object.
  5. Transferred each shape onto the matching value of Fadeless paper.
  6. Cut and glued each value into place on the background color.

2024

Colored pencil and cut-paper collage · 11 × 17 in

I’ve always been interested in how quickly people form opinions about others based on what they see. In this piece, fragments of a woman’s face are surrounded by disconnected shapes, textures, and colors. Some details are immediately recognizable, while others are difficult to make out. Growing up between different cultures taught me that people often see only parts of who we are. Through this composition, I wanted to explore how easily we mistake those fragments for the whole person.

Acrylic on board · 9 × 12 in

Growing up, I often found myself trying to blend in with the people around me. With this painting, I wanted to do the opposite. I used vibrant shades of orange against a dark background to make ordinary objects impossible to overlook. Through this contrast, I explored something I’ve slowly learned about myself: standing out isn’t always a bad thing, and sometimes it’s what makes something interesting.

Cut paper on drawing paper · 12 × 18 in

I’ve always been curious about the rules people follow without questioning them. In this composition, I experimented with patterns that each seem to follow their own system. Some are repetitive, while others are unpredictable, yet they all come together in one design. I wanted to see how much variety I could create using only black and white, and whether something could feel organized and chaotic at the same time.

Graphite on drawing paper · 12 × 18 in

I’ve learned that things don’t always have to fit perfectly to work together. While drawing these overlapping geometric forms, I was interested in how objects with completely different shapes could create a balanced composition. I used shading and perspective to give each form its own space while connecting them into something larger. It reminded me that balance doesn’t always mean everything has to be the same.

Watercolor on paper

I’ve always been fascinated by old objects and the stories they might hold. These two vintage lanterns made me wonder about the places they’ve been and the people who once relied on their light.
